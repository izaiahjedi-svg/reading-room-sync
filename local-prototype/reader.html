Chapter 142 - Names That Burn Quietly

By dusk, the market had already forgotten the fight, but the stone under Kira's boots still held heat like a warning. Lantern sellers closed their stalls one by one, each wick leaving a clean stripe of smoke in the blue air.

He checked the folded map again. The ink looked ordinary until moonlight touched it, then alleys appeared where no street should be, and a red mark pulsed near the river steps. The kind of mark that meant somebody had spoken a true name where they should not have.

When he reached Candle District, the bells were silent. Too silent. Even the stray cats kept to the roofs, watching him like witnesses who had agreed not to testify. Kira set his satchel down, pressed two fingers against the nearest wall, and listened.

The city answered in fragments: a breath, a door latch, a woman humming from three floors up. Then one word rose clear above everything else. His own.

The canal lamps woke in sequence, ten at a time, until the water looked stitched together from soft amber thread. On the far side, a paper shrine burned itself to ash without flame, and the ash rose instead of falling.

Kira stepped into the courtyard and counted the stones. Twelve smooth. One split. The split one had not been there yesterday. He touched it with the side of his hand and felt a pulse under the granite, steady as a second heartbeat.

Above him, the weather vane turned against the wind. A window opened on the fourth floor, though no one stood there. The latch clicked once, twice, like someone rehearsing a warning they had waited years to deliver.

He lifted the map again. The red mark had moved. Not far. Only to where he stood.
